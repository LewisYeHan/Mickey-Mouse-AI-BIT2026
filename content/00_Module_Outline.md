## Part 1: Software ✅

### [1.1 Operating Systems](Part_1/01_Operating_Systems.md) ✅
What an OS is, kernel vs. shell, CLI vs. GUI. OS history and types, and a comparison of Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS. How an OS works: processes and process states, system calls, scheduling, deadlocks, memory, I/O and security. Practice questions with answers.

### [1.2 System Software](Part_1/02_System_Software.md) ✅
System vs. application software, firmware, BIOS vs. UEFI, and the boot process from power button to login screen. Kernel types, device drivers (with a printing example), utilities, and translators (assembler, compiler, interpreter). Practice questions with answers.

### [1.3 Application Software](Part_1/03_Application_Software.md) ✅
User space vs. kernel space, the 5-step execution lifecycle, and system calls. The 8 types of applications, and native vs. web vs. cross-platform apps. Practice questions with answers.

### [1.4 Summary and Quiz](Part_1/Part_1_Summary_and_Quiz.md) ✅
How Sections 1.1 to 1.3 connect, key ideas from each section, and a 13-question quiz covering recall and application, with an answer key.

### [1.5 Glossary](Part_1/Part_1_Glossary.md) ✅
Key terms from Sections 1.1 to 1.3, organized alphabetically, with the section where each term is explained.

## Part 2: Symbolic AI 📝

### [2.1 What Is Symbolic AI?](Part_2/04_What_is_Symbolic_AI.md) ✅
Introduction to Symbolic AI, its foundations, Good Old-Fashioned AI (GOFAI), symbols, explicit knowledge, and rule-based decision-making.

### [2.2 Knowledge Representation](Part_2/05_Knowledge_Representation.md) ✅
How AI systems represent and organize knowledge using facts, propositions, propositional logic, logical equivalences (including De Morgan's laws), predicate logic, semantic networks, frames and scripts, rule-based representation, ontologies, and knowledge graphs, with real-world applications.

### [2.3 How Symbolic AI Reasons](Part_2/06_How_Symbolic_AI_Reasons.md) ✅
How AI derives conclusions from facts and rules. Covers logical inference, Modus Ponens, Modus Tollens, forward chaining, backward chaining, unification, resolution, and reasoning under uncertainty.

### [2.4 Expert Systems](Part_2/07_Expert_Systems.md) ✅
How expert systems work and how they are built. Covers the knowledge base, inference engine, working memory, explanation facility, knowledge acquisition, and examples such as MYCIN, DENDRAL, and XCON.

### [2.5 Strengths and Limitations](Part_2/08_Strengths_and_Limits.md) ✅
The advantages and limitations of Symbolic AI, including explainability, consistency, knowledge acquisition bottlenecks, ambiguity, and scalability. Compares Symbolic AI with Machine Learning and introduces Neuro-Symbolic AI.

### [2.6 Planning and Problem Solving](Part_2/09_Planning_and_Problem_Solving.md) 📝
How AI finds a sequence of actions that reaches a goal. Covers states, actions with preconditions and effects, uninformed search (BFS, DFS, uniform-cost search), informed search (greedy best-first, A\*), adversarial search (minimax, alpha-beta pruning) and local search (hill climbing, simulated annealing), using a campus map example.

### [2.7 NLP in Symbolic AI](Part_2/10_NLP_in_Symbolic_AI.md) 📝
How symbolic methods help computers understand human language. Covers syntax with context-free and phrase structure grammars, semantics in logic, semantic networks and ontologies, rule-based NLP (part-of-speech tagging, parsing, information extraction), logic and inference, sentiment analysis, question answering and information retrieval.

### [2.8 Module Summary and Self-Check Quiz](Part_2/Part_2_Summary_and_Quiz.md) 📝
Connects the seven sections of Part 2, reviews their key ideas, and provides a 15-question self-check quiz covering knowledge recall and practical application, with explanations in the answer key.

### [2.9 Glossary](Part_2/Part_2_Glossary.md) 📝
Key terminology from Sections 2.1 to 2.7, organized into eight categories: Foundations of Symbolic AI, Knowledge Representation, Logical Reasoning, Uncertainty and Reasoning Methods, Expert Systems, AI Approaches and Evaluation, Planning and Search, and Natural Language Processing.
